1. Why own your AI

Owning your AI means the model runs on your machine, so nobody can change its price, its rules or what it does with your words. You take on one job in return: deciding what goes into it.

Rented AI (app or API)Your own AI
Who runs the modelA vendorYou, on your laptop
Who sees your promptsThe vendorNobody else
Who can change the rulesThe vendor, any timeYou
CostSubscription or per useFree after setup
Who checks what goes inThe vendor's safety teamYou, with RA(I)
Quality todayBest availableGood for everyday tasks, weaker on hard ones

Honest trade: an open model on a laptop is not as strong as the best rented models yet. For writing, summaries, notes and private documents it is often enough, and it improves every few months.

2. Pick a model for your machine

Your computer's memory (RAM) decides the model size; start one size below the limit so the machine stays usable.

Your RAMModel size to start withGood for
8 GB3-4B parametersShort answers, simple summaries
16 GB7-8B parametersEveryday writing, notes, document Q&A
32 GB12-14B parametersLonger documents, better reasoning
64 GB or more30B+ parametersClose to entry-level rented models

Default picks, checked against the Ollama library on 29 Oct 2026: 8 GB granite4.2:3b, 16 GB gemma4:e4b (6.6 GB download), 32 GB gemma4:12b. Model names change every few months, so check ollama.com/library before you pull.

Where to find your RAM: on a Mac, Apple menu > About This Mac; on Windows, Settings > System > About.

3. Install: Ollama + Open WebUI

Two programs, one path, no Docker: Ollama runs the model, Open WebUI gives you a chat window in your browser.

  1. Install Ollama from ollama.com and open it once.
  2. Download your model in a terminal: ollama pull gemma4:e4b (or the pick for your RAM from step 2).
  3. Install uv, a small tool runner: on a Mac brew install uv; otherwise follow docs.astral.sh/uv.
  4. Start Open WebUI, reachable only from your own computer:
uvx --python 3.11 open-webui@0.12.5 serve --host 127.0.0.1 --port 3000
  1. Open http://localhost:3000 and create the first account. That account is the admin, so make it yours.

The first start takes a few minutes while it downloads. Tested on 29 Oct 2026: Open WebUI 0.12.5 on a Mac (Apple Silicon) found Ollama at its default address with no extra settings.

Windows: not tested yet. We haven't run this path on a Windows PC ourselves. If you try it, tell us whether it worked (or where it broke) at go@withrai.xyz. You'll get our thanks, a credit in the next version if you want one, and a well-earned high five. ✋

4. Lock the basics

Your AI should only answer to your own computer; four settings get you there.

WhatDo thisWhy
Localhost onlyKeep --host 127.0.0.1 in the start command; leave Ollama's OLLAMA_HOST setting aloneOtherwise anyone on your Wi-Fi, or the internet, can talk to your AI
No open portsDon't forward ports 3000 or 11434 on your routerExposed AI servers are actively scanned for and misused
Pinned versionsStart a fixed release (open-webui@0.12.5), never an unpinned latestA bad update can't slip in silently
Updates on purposeCheck for updates monthly, read the release notes, then change the numberYou decide when new code runs

Want your AI on your phone too? See step 7, never open ports for it.

5. Feed it safely

The biggest risk to your own AI is not a hacker but a document: text that looks harmless to you can carry instructions your AI will follow.

How hidden instructions get in

  • PDFs and Word files with white-on-white or tiny text
  • Web pages your AI reads, with text hidden from view
  • Emails and shared notes you paste in
  • Plugins, tools and "skills" you install from strangers

What never goes into memory or a knowledge base

  • Passwords, API keys, card or ID numbers
  • Documents you haven't read yourself
  • Anything that tells the AI how to behave, unless you wrote it

Simple habits

  • Keep chats with untrusted documents separate from your main memory
  • Read a file before you let your AI learn from it
  • If your AI suddenly behaves differently, check what you added last

Why this matters more over time: a bad note can be deleted, but once your AI learns from something, the instruction can become part of how it thinks.

7. Use it on your phone and other devices

One computer at home runs your AI; your phone, tablet and second laptop connect to it privately, so all devices share the same AI and the same memory.

SetupHowGood for
Home machine + private networkJoin every device to a private network tool (for example Tailscale), share Open WebUI only inside that network, open it from your phone's browserOne AI, same memory everywhere, nothing exposed to the internet
On the phone itselfA small model in an on-device appOffline use; weaker model, separate memory
BothPhone app offline, home AI when connectedTravel

How to do it with Tailscale

  1. Install Tailscale on the home computer and on your phone; sign in with the same account on both.
  2. The first time, Tailscale asks you to switch on "Serve" for your network: one click on the link it shows. Serve keeps it private to your own devices; the public option, Funnel, stays off.
  3. On the home computer, with Open WebUI running: tailscale serve --bg 3000. It prints your private address, like https://<computer-name>.<your-network>.ts.net.
  4. On the phone, with Tailscale on, open that address.

Check it is private: turn Tailscale off on the phone and reload; the page must not load. Tested on 29 Oct 2026 with a Mac and an Android phone.

Rules that keep it safe

  • Never open router ports to reach your AI from outside; the private network replaces that
  • Each device signs in with its own Open WebUI account, so you can remove a lost phone
  • The home machine must be on and awake for other devices to reach it

8. Checklist

  • I know my RAM and picked a model one size below the limit
  • Ollama is installed and my model is downloaded
  • Open WebUI runs at http://localhost:3000 and I own the admin account
  • The start command uses 127.0.0.1 and a pinned version
  • Ollama is not set to 0.0.0.0, and no router ports are open
  • I read files before my AI learns from them
  • No passwords, keys or ID numbers in memory
  • Other devices reach my AI through a private network, not open ports
  • I'm on the list for RA(I)'s intake check
